# Remaining scope: prioritized, with decisions and acceptance criteria

Compiled 2026-10-20 and validated the same day against three sources: the original master specification (`Intuit_Software_Clone_Master_Project_Context_v0.1.pdf`, sections 1 to 36, read in full), `docs/master-spec-coverage.md` (28 rows that are PARTIAL, NOT DONE, NEEDS DECISION or BLOCKED, plus 4 DEFERRED rows), and `docs/requirements-matrix.md` (rows FP-22 to FP-31 that are PARTIAL or BLOCKED). Nothing has been dropped from scope; an item leaves this list only when its acceptance criteria pass **and** the matrix row says so. Having acceptance criteria written here does not make anything complete.

**Counts (after validation).** 6 specified (Tier 1), 20 in Tier 2 of which 19 still need an owner decision (R-11 was decided on 2026-10-21 and is done), 7 blocked on outside input (Tier 3), 6 built or written but unverified in the required environment (Tier 4), 4 deferred by the specification or the owner (Tier 5). The first version of this file counted 6, 15, 6 and 6; validation added R-25, R-26, R-27 and R-28 (decisions), later R-29 (2026-10-20, the meaning of expense by employee), R-46 (blocked) and the Tier 5 section, and moved R-06 from "done" to "in verification" and, after run 6, to done. R-27 and R-28 record what ADR 0015 deliberately left out of the reversible defaults, so those omissions stay visible.

**How to read it.**

- _Suggested default_ is a proposal for the product owner. **It is not applied and not approved.** The column "Owner decision" stays empty until the owner records one; an item is only built once that column is filled (specification section 29: an UNDECIDED requirement must never be silently turned into a design decision).
- _Status_ words: SPECIFIED (the specification says enough to build), DECISION (the owner must choose first), BLOCKED (needs an outside source or credentials), UNVERIFIED (built or written but not run in the required environment), DEFERRED (the specification or owner postponed it; architectural readiness must be kept).
- Acceptance criteria are written to be checkable by an automated test or a named manual check.
- Priority order is by dependency first (what unblocks other work), then risk (accounting correctness, security, statutory compliance), then user value.
